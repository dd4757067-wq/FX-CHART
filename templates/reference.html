1. Candle Anatomy

A candlestick records Open, High, Low and Close (OHLC) for a selected timeframe.

  • Body: distance between open and close.
  • Upper wick: highest traded price above the body.
  • Lower wick: lowest traded price below the body.
  • Bullish: close above open.
  • Bearish: close below open.

How a candle forms: price moves between buyers and sellers during the selected period. At the end, the last traded price becomes the close and a new candle begins.

2. Major Candlestick Patterns

Doji

Open and close are near each other; often shows balance or indecision. Context is essential.

Hammer

Small body near the top with a long lower wick. Can show rejection of lower prices after a decline.

Inverted Hammer

Small body with long upper wick after a decline. Possible bullish clue when confirmed.

Shooting Star

Small body near the low with long upper wick after an advance. Possible bearish rejection.

Hanging Man

Hammer-like candle after an advance; can warn of selling pressure and needs confirmation.

Bullish Engulfing

A bullish body engulfs the previous bearish body. Stronger at meaningful support/liquidity.

Bearish Engulfing

A bearish body engulfs the previous bullish body. Stronger near resistance/liquidity.

Morning Star

Three-candle bullish reversal structure: bearish candle, small middle candle, strong bullish candle.

Evening Star

Three-candle bearish reversal structure: bullish candle, small middle candle, strong bearish candle.

Inside Bar

A candle whose range is inside the previous candle; often represents compression.

Pin Bar

Long wick and small body showing rejection. Direction depends on location and context.

Marubozu

Large body with little/no wick; shows strong directional participation.

3. Classic Chart Patterns

Double Top

Two highs around a similar level. Neckline break can confirm a bearish structure.

Double Bottom

Two lows around a similar level. Neckline break can confirm a bullish structure.

Head & Shoulders

Three-peak reversal structure with a higher middle peak; neckline is key confirmation.

Inverse H&S

Three-trough bullish reversal structure; neckline break is common confirmation.

Ascending Triangle

Flat resistance with rising lows. Watch the breakout, but false breaks can occur.

Descending Triangle

Flat support with falling highs. Watch the breakdown, but false breaks can occur.

Symmetrical Triangle

Lower highs and higher lows create compression; wait for directional confirmation.

Falling Wedge

Descending converging boundaries; often watched as bullish reversal/continuation after confirmation.

Rising Wedge

Rising converging boundaries; often watched as a bearish warning after confirmation.

Flag

Short consolidation after an impulse; continuation breakout is commonly monitored.

Pennant

Small symmetrical consolidation after an impulse; directional breakout is the trigger to watch.

Rectangle

Price oscillates between horizontal boundaries; range reactions or breakout can be monitored.

4. Market Structure

  • HH: Higher High. HL: Higher Low.
  • LH: Lower High. LL: Lower Low.
  • BOS: Break of Structure.
  • CHOCH/MSS: commonly used to describe a potential change in directional structure.
  • Liquidity: areas where orders/stops may cluster, often around obvious highs/lows.
  • Displacement: strong directional price movement, often leaving inefficient price areas.

5. SMC / Price Action Essentials

Liquidity Sweep

Price trades beyond an obvious high/low and then rejects or reverses. Confirm with structure.

Fair Value Gap (FVG)

Three-candle imbalance concept where rapid movement leaves an inefficient zone.

Order Block

Common price-action zone associated with institutional-order narratives; not a guarantee.

Premium / Discount

Relative location inside a dealing range; combine with structure and liquidity.

Inducement

A move that may encourage traders into positions before price seeks liquidity elsewhere.

Mitigation

A return into a prior area where unfilled orders or imbalance are believed to exist.

6. Multi-Timeframe Analysis

Top-down: Higher-timeframe bias → middle-timeframe structure → lower-timeframe execution.

  1. Monthly/Weekly for broad context when appropriate.
  2. Daily/4H for major structure and zones.
  3. 1H/15m for setup formation and liquidity.
  4. 5m/1m only when the strategy requires lower-timeframe execution.
  5. If timeframes strongly conflict, reduce risk or wait.

7. Risk Management

  • Define risk before entry.
  • Size positions from stop distance and acceptable account risk.
  • Use a written invalidation point.
  • Never increase size just to recover a loss.
  • Journal and review a meaningful sample of trades.
  • Past performance does not guarantee future results.

8. Pre-Entry Checklist

  1. Market and session.
  2. Higher-timeframe bias.
  3. Current structure.
  4. Nearby liquidity.
  5. Key support/resistance or supply/demand.
  6. Candle/pattern confirmation.
  7. News/event risk.
  8. Entry, stop, target and position size.
  9. Risk/reward and invalidation.
  10. Is this an A-quality setup or a forced trade?
